We have a custom ICP point-cloud registrar at /app/icp_register.py that aligns a source scan to a target scan. CLI is `python3 /app/icp_register.py <source.npy> <target.npy> <output_pose.txt>` where inputs are .npy files containing Nx3 float64 point sets and output is a 4x4 homogeneous transform (source to target) written row-major as four floats per line. A reference binary `refreg` is on PATH with identical usage `refreg <source.npy> <target.npy> <output_pose.txt>` and no source. Both support `--verbose` that prints per-iteration diagnostics with schema `iter, n_corr, keep_pct, robust_cost, step_scale` — useful to diff the two trajectories side-by-side. Sample pairs are under /app for debugging.

On high-overlap clean pairs the two registrars agree, but on low-overlap or outlier-heavy pairs your current icp_register.py converges to a different pose than refreg — rotation off by a fraction of a degree and translation by several millimeters. The pose looks valid and overlaps visually, but comparison reveals the divergence to a shifted fixed point. The gap comes from an adaptive rejection policy coupled to convergence dynamics, not a single threshold.
